# Genre playbooks

Starting points, not templates. For every design, name what makes this one different (a twist on the verb, a theme, a social mechanic); a genre game with no twist competes head-on with the established leaders. Describe genres by mechanics, never by copying a specific existing game's name, characters, art or levels.

Each playbook: **fantasy · core loop · first 60 s · what keeps them · pitfalls**.

## Obby (obstacle course)

- **Fantasy**: mastery, "I beat it". Often themed (escape a place, climb a tower).
- **Core loop**: attempt a section → fail fast/respawn → succeed → checkpoint → next section.
- **First 60 s**: three easy jumps that teach the controls without text, first checkpoint within 20 s, a visible far goal (the top of the tower).
- **Keeps them**: stage counter and percent complete, saved progress, new mechanics every few stages (teach → test → twist), themed zones, end reward (badge, title, trail), leaderboards for speed or stage.
- **Pitfalls**: difficulty spikes (track deaths per stage), long walks between checkpoints, invisible kill parts, tuning only on keyboard (phones can't do precision jumps as easily), skip-stage purchases used to mask bad tuning.

## Tycoon

- **Fantasy**: owning and growing a business/base.
- **Core loop**: collect income → buy the next build button → income grows → buy more; automate, then expand.
- **First 60 s**: claim a plot instantly (auto-assign the nearest free one), first purchase within 15 s, first building appears with a satisfying build animation.
- **Keeps them**: the plot visibly growing, upgrade paths with choices, rebirth for multipliers, decorating, visiting others' bases, offline income (capped).
- **Pitfalls**: walking back and forth to collect (add auto-collect or a collector near the buttons), buttons too far apart, purchases that don't change anything visible, plots lost on leave (save them).

## Simulator (collect/upgrade/hatch)

- **Fantasy**: getting stronger, collecting rare things.
- **Core loop**: perform the verb (click, swing, mine) → currency → upgrade tool/pets → stronger verb → new area.
- **First 60 s**: the verb in 5 s with big feedback, first upgrade by 45 s, first egg/pet by 2–3 min.
- **Keeps them**: rarity tiers and showing off, new worlds behind price gates, rebirths, quests, events with exclusive items, trading.
- **Pitfalls**: flat feedback (needs big juicy numbers and effects), inflation, too many currencies, pay-to-win pressure, overwhelming UI on phones (progressive disclosure).

## Round-based (PvP, social deduction, last-one-standing)

- **Fantasy**: winning against others, outsmarting friends.
- **Core loop**: lobby → round (role/team, objective) → result → rewards → next round.
- **First 60 s**: join a round in progress or start one within 15 s; while waiting, the lobby has something to do and the countdown is visible. New players' first round should be forgiving (bots, helper hints, protected role).
- **Keeps them**: short rounds (2–6 min), visible stats and ranks, cosmetic unlocks, varied maps and modifiers, friends.
- **Pitfalls**: waiting for enough players (support small player counts, fill with bots or smaller modes), long dead time after dying (spectate with something to do, quick next round), snowballing (comeback mechanics), unclear win condition.

## Battlegrounds / fighting

- **Fantasy**: feeling powerful, mastering a character's moves.
- **Core loop**: pick a moveset → fight → learn combos → win → unlock characters/moves.
- **First 60 s**: a safe training dummy or bot zone next to spawn, 3–4 moves with clear inputs on screen (and big touch buttons), first knockout vs a dummy in 30 s.
- **Keeps them**: move mastery, character variety, kill streaks, ranks, cosmetics (auras, emotes).
- **Pitfalls**: veterans farming newcomers (spawn protection, safe zones, level-based servers), unreadable combat (clear wind-ups, hit feedback; see the roblox-vfx and roblox-animation skills), latency unfairness (server-validated hits with client-side feel, lag compensation within limits).

## Tower defense

- **Fantasy**: clever strategy, collecting units.
- **Core loop**: place towers → wave → earn → upgrade/place more → next wave; between matches unlock/summon units.
- **First 60 s**: a guided first wave where placing two towers wins; clear path visualization; speed-up button.
- **Keeps them**: unit collection with roles, harder modes, co-op with friends, evolving units, events.
- **Pitfalls**: long early waves (add speed controls), unclear tower ranges (show them while placing), power creep making old units useless, gacha pressure.

## Horror / story / escape

- **Fantasy**: fear and curiosity with friends.
- **Core loop**: explore → find clue/item → solve → threat escalates → escape the chapter.
- **First 60 s**: atmosphere immediately (lighting, sound), a clear first objective, a scare or strange event by 60 s.
- **Keeps them**: chapters released over time, secrets and multiple endings, co-op roles, replay variants (randomized item spots, monster behaviour).
- **Pitfalls**: too dark to see on phones (use contrast and silhouettes, not pitch black), the same scare repeated, unclear objectives (players wander and quit), punishing solo players in a game designed for groups.

## Roleplay / hangout

- **Fantasy**: living another life, being with friends.
- **Core loop**: choose a role/house/job → interact with others and the world → earn/decorate → express.
- **First 60 s**: spawn into a lively town with a clear "get a house / pick a job / grab a car" action, and other players nearby.
- **Keeps them**: self-expression (houses, outfits, vehicles), jobs with simple loops, events, friends.
- **Pitfalls**: empty servers (merge small servers, set max players right), nothing to do alone, griefing (moderation tools, private property), unsafe social features (use Roblox's text filtering for all player text and follow its chat/policy rules).

## Survival / crafting

- **Fantasy**: building something from nothing, surviving together.
- **Core loop**: gather → craft → build/defend → survive the threat (night, wave, hunger) → expand.
- **First 60 s**: gather and craft the first tool within a minute, guided by a crafting quest pill.
- **Keeps them**: base building, tech tree, co-op, seasons/wipes for fresh starts.
- **Pitfalls**: recipe complexity shown too early (unlock recipes gradually), losing a base while offline without warning, tedious gathering (scale yields with tools).

## RPG / adventure

- **Fantasy**: becoming a hero, exploring a world.
- **Core loop**: quest → fight/explore → loot/XP → level up/gear → harder area.
- **First 60 s**: a short action-packed intro quest, first fight and first loot in under a minute, the overworld's landmark visible.
- **Keeps them**: story, bosses, gear builds, party play, dungeons.
- **Pitfalls**: dialogue walls, slow early levelling, empty open worlds (density over size), heavy content cost (needs a reusable content pipeline).

## Racing / driving

- **Fantasy**: speed, cool vehicles.
- **Core loop**: race/drive → earn → buy/upgrade vehicles → faster/new tracks.
- **First 60 s**: in a car and moving within 10 s; a short race or driving challenge; vehicle handling that feels good on touch.
- **Keeps them**: vehicle collection and customization, time trials and leaderboards, friends.
- **Pitfalls**: physics jank (network ownership to the driver, tuned suspension), bad touch controls, long walks to the garage.

## Minigames / party

- **Fantasy**: quick fun with friends and strangers.
- **Core loop**: short random minigame → winners earn → cosmetics/unlocks → next minigame.
- **First 60 s**: immediately in or about to start a minigame; rules shown in one line plus a visual.
- **Keeps them**: variety, surprise, social laughs, cosmetics.
- **Pitfalls**: weak minigames (cut them using play/vote data), rules too complex for one line, waiting between games.
